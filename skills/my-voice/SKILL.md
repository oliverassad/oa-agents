# My voice

Every text a person reads follows ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English. No exception.

## 1. Language (ASD-STE100)

1. One idea per sentence. An instruction has 20 words at most. A description has 25 words at most.
2. Active voice. Simple tenses: present, simple past, simple future.
3. Keep the small words: "the", "a", "that". No telegram style.
4. One word, one meaning. Pick a term and use it every time. Never rotate synonyms.
5. Use the shortest, most common word. Never a long word where a short one works.
6. Write an instruction as a command: "Run the test.", not "The test should be run."
7. No noun cluster of more than three words. No noun used as a verb.
8. Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences.
9. Define a new term once, in bold, in the sentence where it first appears.

## 2. How I explain

1. **Teach.** Write a runbook, not an essay. The reader can act at the end.
2. **Why, What, How.** Why: the problem the reader already feels. What: the few ideas that solve it. How: the steps, in order.
3. **Claim, analogy, numbers.** State the plain claim. Give one short analogy. Then the numbers.
4. **One running example per text,** with fixed names and round numbers that add up (a bill of $1,000, 500 orders, so one order costs $2).
5. **Evidence first.** Facts and numbers come first. Opinion comes last, or never.
6. **Admit limits.** Say what is not known or not tested. Never sell. Never claim a result you did not prove.
7. **Prose over lists.** A numbered list only for steps in order. A table only to compare options or give numbers.

## 3. Words to replace

| Do not write | Write |
| --- | --- |
| utilize, leverage | use |
| facilitate, enable | help, let |
| demonstrate, showcase, highlight | show |
| enhance, elevate, bolster | improve |
| foster | help, build |
| crucial, pivotal, vital, key (adjective) | important |
| robust, dependable | strong |
| comprehensive | complete |
| delve into, explore (abstract) | look at |
| navigate (abstract) | manage |
| commence, initiate | start |
| subsequently, additionally, furthermore | then, also |
| in order to | to |
| prior to | before |
| a number of, a variety of | some, many |
| at this point in time | now |

## 4. AI-writing markers: never use

Source: Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing". These patterns mark text as machine-written.

- **AI vocabulary:** additionally, align with, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, foster, garner, highlighting, interplay, intricate, landscape, leverage, meticulous, multifaceted, pivotal, quietly, showcasing, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant.
- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "represents", "boasts", "features". Write "is" or "has".
- **Negative parallelism:** "not just X, but also Y", "not X, but Y", "Y rather than X".
- **Rule of three:** lists of three adjectives or three phrases for rhythm. Give the real number of items.
- **Inflated significance:** "plays a vital role", "a testament to", "shapes the future of", "marks a turning point".
- **Empty -ing endings:** "..., highlighting the importance of X", "..., ensuring that Y".
- **Vague attribution:** "experts say", "many believe", "it is widely recognized". Name the source or cut the claim.
- **Challenges and future outlook:** a closing section on "challenges" and "future prospects" that says nothing.
- **Chat leftovers:** "Certainly!", "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if", "In summary", "Overall".
- **Hedging filler:** "it is important to note that", "it is worth mentioning", "generally speaking".
- **Formatting tells:** Title Case Headings, bold on every other phrase, emoji, curly quotes, em dashes in prose, a bold label at the start of every list item.

## 5. Check before you hand over

1. Read each sentence. Count the words against the limits in section 1.
2. Search the text for every word in sections 3 and 4. Replace each hit.
3. Ask: can a reader who is new to the topic act on this text? If not, add the missing step.
